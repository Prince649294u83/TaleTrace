The lighthouse had been dark for as long as anyone in the village could remember. Mira climbed the cliff path every morning to look at it. Her grandmother said the light went out the year the harbour froze. Nobody had gone inside since.

"You'll fall," her brother called from the rocks below. Mira did not answer him. The door at the top of the path was swollen shut with salt and rain, and it took both hands to move it. Inside, the air smelled of cold iron.

A spiral staircase wound up into the dark. Dr. Alder, the village physician, had told her once that the keeper simply walked away one night. He left his coat on the hook. He left his tea going cold on the table.

Mira counted one hundred and twelve steps to the top. The lamp room was smaller than she expected. Glass panes curved around a brass mechanism the size of a rowing boat, and every surface was furred with dust.

"Hello?" she said. Her voice came back to her from the glass. There was no keeper, of course. There was only the lamp, and beside it, a logbook left open as though someone had stepped out for a moment.

The last entry was dated forty years ago. It read: the light does not go out, it only waits. Mira turned the page and found the rest of the book empty. Outside, the sea moved against the rocks the way it always had.

She found the mechanism stiff but not broken. It wanted oil and it wanted turning. She worked until her arms ached and the sun went down behind the headland, and then, with a sound like a held breath released, the lamp caught.

Down in the village, one by one, people came out of their houses to look up. The light swept out across the water, steady and unhurried. Her brother stood on the rocks with his mouth open. Mira, at the top of the tower, was already laughing.
